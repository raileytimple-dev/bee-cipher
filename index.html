04 / ABOUT BEE CIPHER

Inspired by a buzz.
Built around a key.

Bee Cipher is your custom quaternary cipher. It combines a deterministic ASCII shuffle, a four-branch Huffman codebook, and key-controlled cyclic symbol substitutions.

Meet the researchers & explore the study ↓
Why only b, z, B and Z?

Four symbols form a quaternary alphabet. Each branch of the tree uses one symbol. Case matters: lowercase b and uppercase B are different symbols.

How does decryption work?

Recreate the tree and symbol cycle using the same key. Undo the flip passes in reverse order with the inverse cycle, then walk the tree to recover characters. As in your Python code, decoded ASCII control characters display as “?”. A wrong key is not reliably detectable.

Do repeated key letters matter?

Yes. “leoleo” and “leo” share intervals 12, 5, 15, but their complete SHA-256 hashes differ. Their character assignments can therefore differ. The four-symbol cycle has only 24 possible permutations, so different keys can share a flip order.

What do the results tell us about security?

This is an educational custom cipher, without an independent security assessment. Balanced symbol counts, SHA-256 key hashing, and a large theoretical key space do not establish encryption strength. The implemented shuffle retains 64 bits of state; actual resistance also depends on key choice and the cipher’s structure. Use established authenticated encryption for sensitive data.

THE STUDY AT A GLANCE

The team implemented Bee Cipher in Python and compared it with Caesar, monoalphabetic substitution, and Vigenère. The reported benchmark used a 941-character plaintext, the key worldpeace, and 2,000 iterations per runtime measurement.

The design draws inspiration from Morse code’s compact symbol alphabet, extending the idea to four symbols with a key-dependent Huffman tree and positional substitutions.

01 / UNDERSTAND THE METRICS

Read beyond the percentage.

Shannon entropy measures symbol distribution: H = −Σ p log₂ p. For four symbols, the maximum is 2 bits per symbol. Normalized entropy divides H by that maximum.

The study’s avalanche metric compares symbols, rather than individual bits. Two independent, uniformly random four-symbol streams differ at about 75% of positions; the familiar 50% benchmark applies to bit differences. Entropy and sensitivity alone do not establish security.

02 / THE TRADEOFFS

More symbols. Limited diffusion.

The paper reports 3,323 output symbols from 941 input characters. Storing each b, z, B or Z as a text character creates roughly 3.53× expansion for that sample.

A plaintext change altered only 0.06% of the output in the reported test. This illustrates limited plaintext diffusion, even though a key change affected much more of the stream.

03 / FUTURE DIRECTIONS

Where the team wants to go.

The paper proposes a cryptographically secure generator such as ChaCha20 or AES-CTR for key scheduling, an extra transposition or XOR stage for diffusion, and alternative encoding choices to reduce expansion.

These are research proposals. The simulator demonstrates the supplied Python algorithm, with its original key scheduling and flip passes.
